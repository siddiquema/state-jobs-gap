# AISHE data-quality check: West Bengal UG pass-outs

Source: AISHE Table 33 (out-turn, "based on actual response") and Table 1(a) (response), 2019-20 to 2023-24.

| Year | WB institutions responded / registered | WB response | WB UG pass-outs | National UG (sum of states) | WB share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019-20 | 1,933 / 1,935 | 99.9% | 302,453 | 6,650,071 | 4.5% |
| 2020-21 | 2,020 / 2,025 | 99.8% | 400,113 | 6,909,846 | 5.8% |
| 2021-22 | 2,139 / 2,146 | 99.7% | 410,187 | 7,754,223 | 5.3% |
| 2022-23 | 2,243 / 2,249 | 99.7% | 424,345 | 7,914,391 | 5.4% |
| 2023-24 | 2,331 / 2,435 | 95.7% | 348,424 | 7,775,555 | 4.5% |

## Findings
- Response rate does **not** explain the 2023-24 fall. WB response dropped only ~4 points (standalone institutions 88%; colleges 99.2%), while UG pass-outs fell 18%.
- National UG pass-outs fell only ~2% in 2023-24, so the drop is specific to West Bengal.
- WB's share of national UG pass-outs is ~5.3-5.8% in 2020-21 to 2022-23 but 4.5% in 2019-20 and 2023-24.
- Cause not established. Candidates: real decline, a change in how WB institutions report pass-outs (e.g. universities with large affiliated-college or open/distance cohorts), or a reporting-lag effect. Needs checking against Table 36 (UG pass-outs by discipline) and the WB programme-level table (Table 34).

## Rule for the report
Show the five-year series with this caveat, do not present the 2023-24 drop as a real fall, and use a multi-year average (2020-21 to 2022-23 ~ 411k, or all five ~ 377k) as the supply baseline until the cause is found.
